                30 MARKS RUBRIC ALIGNED

                1. Problem Statement & User Personas

                Target Users: University students and travel groups (e.g., NUST students on a northern areas trip to Hunza/Skardu) who frequently share food, fuel, tickets, and lodging costs.

                Core Problem: Calculating who owes whom becomes tangled when multiple members pay for different people with uneven splits, leading to awkward debt friction and calculation errors.

                Solution: A zero-latency client-side tracker that calculates net balances, prevents rounding leaks with integer-paisa math, and minimizes transfers using a greedy cash-flow optimizer.

                2. Web Development Life Cycle (WDLC)

                1. Planning: Defined scope for Assignment 1 (client-side only, fictional data, no external payment APIs).
                2. Analysis: Identified core data entities (Members, Expenses, Splits, Settlements, Activity Logs).
                3. Design: Built mobile-first wireframes with 3 responsive breakpoints (320px, 768px, 1100px), semantic HTML tags, and accessible color schemes.
                4. Development: Implemented modular vanilla JS ES6+ without external frameworks or templates.
                5. Testing: Verified zero-sum balance invariant, empty states, XSS sanitization, CSV formula injection defense, and screen-reader accessibility.
                6. Deployment: Configured PWA with Web Manifest and Service Worker for offline execution.

                4. Mathematical Algorithms: Split Distribution & Debt Simplification

                Paisa Integer Rounding: JavaScript floating point issues (e.g. 0.1 + 0.2 !== 0.3) are eliminated by storing all monetary amounts as integer paisas (cents). Equal splits calculate Math.floor(total / n) and distribute remainder paisas to the first r members so the sum of individual shares strictly equals the total bill.

                Greedy Min-Cash-Flow Simplification: Reduces pairwise debts from \(O(N^2)\) to at most \(N - 1\) transfers by iteratively matching the member who owes the most (maximal debtor) with the member who is owed the most (maximal creditor).

                ⚡ How Debt Simplification Works

                When friends travel together, raw pairwise debts can create a tangled web of dozens of small IOUs (e.g., Ali owes Bilal, Bilal owes Alia, Alia owes Ali).

                1. Net Balance Calculation:

                We sum what each member paid minus what each member consumed to get their net financial position (+Rs for creditors, −Rs for debtors). Net balances always sum to zero.

                2. Greedy Bipartite Matching:

                We pair the largest debtor with the largest creditor. The debtor transfers min(|debt|, |credit|) directly to the creditor, resolving or reducing both balances.

                3. Result:

                A group of 4 members requires at most 3 direct transactions instead of 6 complex pairwise debts!
